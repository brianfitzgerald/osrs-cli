## Gather the account state

1. Run `player <user>` for skills and combat data.
2. Run `full <user>` instead when methods may need quests or area access.
3. Reuse that snapshot throughout the response.
4. Check unrendered skills when needed. For example, Sailing may exist in the raw WOM snapshot or WikiSync `levels` even if the CLI skill table omits it.

Treat recommended levels on Wiki guides as performance advice, not hard requirements. Check quest, item, diary, spellbook, and area requirements separately.

## Get the candidate set

Start with `wiki "Money making guide"`. Its hourly-profit table is the canonical broad list. Use the individual page `wiki "Money making guide/<Method>"` for each serious candidate.

The main table is generated by `Template:MMG list`. The CLI Markdown output may omit generated rows or calculated values. If that happens, read the server-rendered table through the MediaWiki API:

```text
https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/api.php?action=parse&page=Money%20making%20guide&prop=text&format=json
```

Filter the rendered rows by the `Intensity` column. Inspect the individual guide's raw wikitext when its assumptions are missing from rendered Markdown:

```text
https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/api.php?action=parse&page=Money%20making%20guide%2F<Method>&prop=wikitext&format=json
```

Use `Money making guide/Recurring` for herb runs, bird houses, kingdom rewards, and other timer-based methods. Keep recurring profit separate from continuous hourly profit.

## Classify attention accurately

The Wiki's `Low` intensity label does not mean AFK. Classify each candidate by its real interaction pattern:

| Class | Typical interaction | Examples of the pattern |
|---|---:|---|
| Long AFK | 2+ minutes | Shipwreck salvaging, regular-mould cannonballs, Shooting Stars |
| Work-friendly | 45-120 seconds | Dart tips, some jewellery, double-mould cannonballs |
| Semi-AFK | 15-45 seconds | Unfinished potions, ultracompost, short processing inventories |
| Low intensity only | Frequent movement or clicks | Shop buying, tanning, orb charging, collecting, combat setup |

State the expected interaction interval. Do not describe a method as AFK only because the Wiki labels it `Low`.

## Validate rates and assumptions

Wiki headline rates can assume unlocks that the player lacks. Inspect the individual guide for:

- equipment and outfit effects;
- diaries and quest rewards;
- optional levels included in the headline rate;
- items gathered earlier and treated as zero-cost;
- alchemy instead of Grand Exchange sales;
- output-dose conversions for potions;
- tick manipulation, alternate accounts, or runners;
- storage items, crew, servants, or house facilities;
- the stated actions per hour.

For example, a profitable potion headline may assume Prescription goggles, an Alchemist's amulet, level 60 Herblore, and a diary service. Recalculate the no-equipment case for a player who lacks them.

## Recalculate current profit

Use `price` for every material input and tradeable output in the shortlisted methods.

Default to a conservative instant-trade calculation:

- buy inputs at `high`;
- sell outputs at `low`;
- apply the current Grand Exchange tax to taxable outputs;
- include consumable charges, service fees, runes, ammunition, and expected losses;
- multiply by the guide's realistic actions per hour for the player's setup.

Do not hard-code the Grand Exchange tax rate. Confirm the current rule or use the Wiki guide's current calculation.

For random loot, use the Wiki's expected value but state that the result has variance. For rare-drop methods, separate ordinary loot from unique-drop value.

Recommend a small test batch for processing methods. Check buy limits and traded volume before suggesting a large purchase. A high theoretical margin is not useful when inputs do not buy or outputs do not sell.

## Match methods to the goal

Calculate the target budget before ranking methods. Include a buffer for price movement, deaths, failed crops, or other risk. Show the expected hours or runs needed:

```text
time to goal = remaining budget / adjusted profit per hour
```

Prefer a slightly slower method when it advances a stated goal, such as:

- Smithing for a quest requirement;
- Sailing for an island unlock;
- Thieving for While Guthix Sleeps;
- combat stats for a boss;
- Herblore or Magic when the method gives relevant experience.

Do not recommend a long unlock grind only to fund a small immediate purchase. Put strong future methods in a separate near-unlock section.

## Present the result

Lead with the best method for the user's attention limit. Then provide:

1. methods available now;
2. actual interaction interval;
3. Wiki profit and adjusted current profit;
4. starting capital and time to the target;
5. useful experience or unlock progress;
6. material assumptions and market risks;
7. strong near-term unlocks;
8. methods that look eligible but are quest-locked or only nominally low intensity.

Use a compact comparison table. Keep exact prices timestamped or identify them as a current snapshot because they change quickly.
